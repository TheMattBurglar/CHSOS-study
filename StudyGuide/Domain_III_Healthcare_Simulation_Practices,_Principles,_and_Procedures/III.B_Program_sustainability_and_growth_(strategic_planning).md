# III.B: Program sustainability and growth (strategic planning)

## Domain: Healthcare Simulation Practices, Principles, and Procedures (25%)

### [Status]: 🟢 Studied

### [Technician Perspective]
**Program Sustainability & Growth:** Collaborating with stakeholders (steering committee, nursing/medical faculty, hospital administration, finance/procurement) is how a **Strategic Plan** gets built and funded, not just written by the specialist alone.
*   **Utilization Rate:** (Actual Scheduled Hours / Total Available Hours) x 100. A healthy rate is 70-85%.
*   **Gap Analysis:** Identifying the difference between current resources and future needs; the evidence base a specialist brings to stakeholder meetings to justify growth.
*   **Redundancy Planning:** Having a backup plan for critical equipment (e.g., a spare manikin or task trainer).
*   **Simulator Purchases:** A major capital decision the specialist informs but rarely makes alone. Build a side-by-side comparison for stakeholders that includes not just sticker price but ongoing costs: annual warranty/service contract fees, consumables (skins, veins, fluid reservoirs), and technology depreciation, versus the learning objectives the simulator serves.

### [Clinical Application]
Sustainability metrics (like utilization and gap analysis) prove the value of the center to stakeholders, justifying continued budget for high-stakes clinical training and giving the strategic plan measurable targets rather than vague goals.

### [Key Terminology]
*   **Utilization Rate:** The measure of resource efficiency.
*   **Gap Analysis:** The process of determining what is missing to reach a goal.
*   **Redundancy:** Intentional duplication of critical system components.
*   **Strategic Plan:** A stakeholder-endorsed document communicating the program's goals (e.g., growth, new modalities) and the actions and resources needed to achieve them, usually on a 3-5 year horizon.
*   **Throughput:** The number of learners a simulation center can train in a given period; a growth metric stakeholders weigh alongside utilization.
*   **Total Cost of Ownership:** Purchase price plus warranty/service, consumables, and staff training costs over a simulator's expected lifespan, used to compare purchase options.

### [Exam Scenario]
**Q:** Leadership asks the simulation specialist to recommend whether to purchase a new high-fidelity manikin or two low-fidelity task trainers with the same budget, as part of next year's strategic plan. What should the specialist bring to the stakeholder meeting?
**A:** A gap analysis mapping the request to actual curriculum needs (which objectives require full-body physiologic response vs. isolated skills practice), plus a total-cost-of-ownership comparison covering warranty/service contract terms, consumables, and utilization projections, so stakeholders can weigh cost against educational impact rather than sticker price alone.
**Q2:** Your center's utilization rate is 98%. Why might this be an operational concern?
**A2:** It indicates a lack of time for preventive maintenance, staff development, and setup/teardown, which increases the risk of technical failure during sessions.
