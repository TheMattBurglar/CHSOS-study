# III.D: Resource utilization and inventory management

## Domain: Healthcare Simulation Practices, Principles, and Procedures (25%)

### [Status]: 🟢 Studied

### [Technician Perspective]
**Resource Utilization & Inventory Management:** Efficient use has two halves: **feasible use of equipment** (matching the right tool to the objective and budget, not defaulting to the most expensive option) and **inventory management** (keeping consumables in stock without overspending).
*   **Feasible Equipment Use:** Select a task trainer instead of a full high-fidelity manikin when the objective is an isolated psychomotor skill (e.g., an IV arm for cannulation practice instead of tying up a $80k manikin); repurpose equipment across courses; weigh DIY builds against vendor purchases when a commercial trainer isn't cost-justified for the training volume.
*   **Operational Math:** Use formulas to prevent stockouts and justify budget.
*   **PAR Level (Periodic Automatic Replenishment):** The minimum quantity of a supply to keep on hand. Formula: (Avg Daily Usage x Lead Time) + Safety Stock.
*   **Lead Time:** Total time from ordering to arrival (e.g., if a part takes 4 weeks to arrive, your reorder point must be higher).
*   **FIFO (First-In, First-Out):** Crucial for perishables like IV fluids or medications (even simulated ones).

### [Clinical Application]
Efficient inventory management ensures that clinical supplies (gauze, tubes, meds) are always available, preventing 'Simulator Artifacts' where a learner is told 'just pretend you have this'.

### [Key Terminology]
*   **PAR Level:** The 'Minimum' stock level required.
*   **Lead Time:** The delay between ordering and receiving goods.
*   **Safety Stock:** The 'Buffer' stock kept for unexpected spikes in demand.
*   **Consumables:** Single-use items (gauze, meds, tape) that must be tracked for budget purposes.
*   **Feasible Use:** Choosing the least complex, lowest-cost equipment that still meets the scenario's learning objectives.

### [Exam Scenario]
**Q:** The lab is frequently running out of IV start kits. What is the operational solution?
**A:** Calculate a **PAR Level** based on your average weekly usage and the supplier's **Lead Time**, then implement a tracking system (like a Kanban card) to trigger reorders.
**Q2:** A faculty member requests the center's one high-fidelity manikin for a session whose only objective is practicing NG tube insertion. Two other courses that week need the manikin for full-code scenarios. What is the resourceful solution?
**A2:** Redirect the NG tube session to a dedicated task trainer (feasible use of equipment matched to the objective), preserving the high-fidelity manikin's limited availability for the sessions that actually require full physiologic response.

### [Notes & Lab Application]
*   Set 'Par Levels' for all high-volume supplies like tape, gloves, and alcohol pads.
