# V.A: Principles of instructional design

## Domain: Concepts in Instructional Design as Applied to Simulation (15%)

### [Status]: 🟢 Studied

### [Technician Perspective]
**Principles of Instructional Design:** Utilize the **ADDIE model**. Techs must identify which phase a problem belongs to. If learners are confused during the brief, it's a *Design/Develop* issue. If the manikin fails during the run, it's an *Implementation* issue.

### [Clinical Application]
Proper instructional design ensures that the simulation is not just a 'tech demo' but a structured educational event. A failure in the 'Analysis' phase (choosing the wrong modality) ruins the clinical outcome.

### [Key Terminology]
*   **ADDIE:** Analyze, Design, Develop, Implement, Evaluate.
*   **Needs Assessment (Analysis):** The 'Why'. Identifying the gap in knowledge.
*   **SME (Subject Matter Expert):** The individual with substantive expertise in the clinical topic area.
*   **PADDIE+M:** An expanded version of ADDIE used for simulation program management: Planning, Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, Evaluation, and Maintenance. The 'M' covers ongoing equipment upkeep and scenario updates after go-live.
*   **Kirkpatrick's Four Levels:** A complementary model for what to measure during ADDIE's 'Evaluate' phase: Reaction (satisfaction), Learning (knowledge/skill gained), Behavior (transfer to practice), and Results (patient outcomes).

### [Exam Scenario]
**[Analysis Level] Q:** During the 'Evaluate' phase of ADDIE, you notice that 80% of learners failed to recognize a simulated MI because the EKG was too blurry on the monitor. Where should the correction occur?
**A:** In the **Development** phase. The technical setup (physical fidelity of the monitor) failed to support the educational objective.
