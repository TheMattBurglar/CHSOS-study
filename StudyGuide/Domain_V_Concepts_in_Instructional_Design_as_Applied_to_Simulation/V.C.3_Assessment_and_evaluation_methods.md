# V.C.3: Assessment and evaluation methods

## Domain: Concepts in Instructional Design as Applied to Simulation (15%)

### [Status]: 🟢 Studied

### [Technician Perspective]
**Assessment and Evaluation Methods:** Use checklists, rubrics, or automated software scoring. Distinguish between Formative (feedback for learning) and Summative (grading for competency). Specialists ensure the technical reliability of these assessment tools.

### [Clinical Application]
Reliable assessment tools ensure that clinical competency is accurately measured before a learner works with real patients.

### [Key Terminology]
*   **Formative Evaluation:** 'Low stakes' feedback given during the learning process.
*   **Summative Evaluation:** 'High stakes' testing at the end of a unit or course.
*   **Kirkpatrick's Four Levels:** A model for what evaluation data actually measures: **Reaction** (post-sim satisfaction survey), **Learning** (skills checklist/written test), **Behavior** (change in real clinical practice), and **Results** (patient outcomes). Most simulation data collected in the lab only reaches Level 1-2; Level 3-4 requires data from outside the sim center.
*   **OSCE (Objective Structured Clinical Examination):** A structured, multi-station summative format where learners rotate through timed stations, each scored against a standardized checklist.

### [Exam Scenario]
**[Analysis Level] Q:** After a course, students report high satisfaction on the post-sim survey, but six months later, chart audits show no improvement in actual bedside handoff quality. Using Kirkpatrick's model, what does this gap tell the specialist?
**A:** The program is succeeding at **Level 1 (Reaction)** but has not been shown to succeed at **Level 3 (Behavior)** — satisfaction does not guarantee transfer of skill to real practice. The specialist should push for Level 2 (a skills checklist during the sim) and Level 3/4 data (unit-based audits) rather than relying on the survey alone to judge the program's effectiveness.
