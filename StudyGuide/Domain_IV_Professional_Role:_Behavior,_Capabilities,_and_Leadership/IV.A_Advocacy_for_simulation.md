# IV.A: Advocacy for simulation

## Domain: Professional Role: Behavior, Capabilities, and Leadership (15%)

### [Status]: 🟢 Studied

### [Technician Perspective]
**Advocacy for Simulation:** Promote the value of simulation by demonstrating ROI (Return on Investment) through data. Use patient safety metrics, cost-avoidance from errors, and learner satisfaction scores to advocate for resources and personnel. Engage in outreach to other departments to expand the center's impact. Seek out **presentations** (conference abstracts, grand rounds, poster sessions) as a way to advocate for the specialist role and the program's outcomes. Advocate through **community involvement**, such as staffing a manikin demo at a hospital health fair or a local school's career day, to build public trust and pipeline future talent. Understand your program's **institutional structure** (where it sits on the org chart, which committees it reports to) since that placement determines who controls the budget and who needs to be convinced.

### [Clinical Application]
Simulation advocacy directly impacts patient care by securing the resources needed to train clinical teams in high-risk, low-frequency procedures.

### [Key Terminology]
*   **ROI (Return on Investment):** The financial or clinical benefit vs. the cost of the program.
*   **Stakeholders:** Individuals or groups with a vested interest in the simulation program (e.g., Dean, Hospital CEO, Nursing Director).
*   **Institutional Structure:** Where the simulation program sits within the organization's reporting hierarchy (e.g., under Nursing Education, GME, or as a standalone department); this placement shapes funding, autonomy, and which stakeholders must approve advocacy efforts.

### [Exam Scenario]
**Q:** You are asked to justify the purchase of a new $80k manikin to the hospital board. What is the most persuasive operational argument?
**A:** Link the manikin's capabilities to a specific clinical safety goal, such as reducing the rate of post-partum hemorrhage complications through regular team training.
