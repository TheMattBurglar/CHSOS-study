# V.C.10: Implementation to participants

## Domain: Concepts in Instructional Design as Applied to Simulation (15%)

### [Status]: 🟢 Studied

### [Technician Perspective]
**Implementation to Participants (the blueprint calls this item 'Set-up'):** Immediately before learners arrive, the specialist performs final **Set-up** — physically staging the room per the Room Setup Map, loading and verifying the scenario's initial state (vitals, moulage, supply bins) against the script, and confirming A/V is recording. This is the last checkpoint before **Implementation**, when the specialist manages the simulator's responses in real-time as participants run the scenario. Crucially, the exam tests 'Priority Logic' once live: 1. Participant/Patient Safety, 2. Educational Objectives, 3. Equipment Preservation.

### [Clinical Application]
Smooth implementation keeps learners immersed. If a real medical emergency occurs during simulation (e.g., a student faints), the tech must immediately break the 'Fiction Contract' and initiate real-world emergency protocols.

### [Key Terminology]
*   **Implementation:** The act of running the simulation for the intended learners.
*   **Set-up:** The final, hands-on staging and verification of the room, equipment, and simulator state performed right before a session begins. Distinct from **Logistics (V.C.5)**, which is the advance planning that produces the tools (room bookings, the Room Setup Map, supply lists) Set-up executes on the day of the session.
*   **Priority Hierarchy:** Safety > Learning Objectives > Equipment.
*   **Simulator Artifact:** An unnatural behavior that reminds the learner they are in a simulation.

### [Exam Scenario]
**Q:** During a live session, the manikin's compressor starts smoking. What is the very first priority action?
**A:** 1. Ensure the physical safety of the learners and staff (evacuate/fire protocol). Do NOT prioritize saving the manikin or pausing the software first.
