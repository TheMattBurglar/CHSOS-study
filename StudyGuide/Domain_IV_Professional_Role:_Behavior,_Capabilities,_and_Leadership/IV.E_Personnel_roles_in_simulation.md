# IV.E: Personnel roles in simulation

## Domain: Professional Role: Behavior, Capabilities, and Leadership (15%)

### [Status]: 🟢 Studied

### [Technician Perspective]
**Personnel Roles in Simulation:** Understand the boundaries between the 'Educator,' 'Facilitator,' 'Operations Specialist,' 'Debriefer,' and 'Embedded Participant' (Confederate). The **Educator** (often a clinical faculty SME) designs the case, sets learning objectives, and owns the curriculum content; the **Facilitator** runs the live session and guides the learning experience (often the same person as the Educator, but not always); the **Operations Specialist** manages the technology and environment that bring the Educator's design to life. Often, the specialist must play multiple roles; clear transition between these roles is vital.

### [Clinical Application]
Techs acting as confederates (e.g., a family member) provide 'Clinical Realism' that can steer a scenario toward its learning objectives.

### [Key Terminology]
*   **Confederate (Embedded Participant):** An individual who plays a role to guide the scenario from the inside.
*   **Educator:** The subject-matter expert (often a nurse or physician faculty member) who designs the scenario's clinical content and learning objectives.
*   **Operations Specialist:** Manages the tech and environment.
*   **Facilitator:** Manages the overall learning experience during the live session.

### [Exam Scenario]
**Q:** The lab is short-staffed, and you are asked to be both the manikin operator and a confederate nurse in the room. What is the risk?
**A:** 'Cognitive Overload' for the operator, leading to delayed manikin responses or missed technical cues, which can break the simulation's fidelity.

**Q:** A new faculty member submits a scenario request but the objectives are vague and the vitals don't match the intended diagnosis. Whose responsibility is it to correct the clinical content, and what is the Operations Specialist's role?
**A:** Correcting clinical content and objectives is the **Educator's** responsibility, not the Operations Specialist's; the specialist should flag the inconsistency and collaborate with the Educator to clarify the objectives before building the technical scenario, rather than guessing at the clinical intent.
