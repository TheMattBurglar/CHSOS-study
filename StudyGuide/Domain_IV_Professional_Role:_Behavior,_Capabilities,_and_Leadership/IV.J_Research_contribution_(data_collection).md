# IV.J: Research contribution (data collection)

## Domain: Professional Role: Behavior, Capabilities, and Leadership (15%)

### [Status]: 🟢 Studied

### [Technician Perspective]
**Research Contribution:** Support **data collection** by exporting software logs, managing video data, and ensuring 'Technical Consistency' for every research subject. If the simulator behaves differently for Group A than Group B, the research is invalid. Beyond collecting data, contribute to **evidence-based practices**: when a peer-reviewed study shows a technique works better (e.g., a specific debriefing model reduces learner anxiety), help pilot and adopt it locally, and consider co-authoring or being acknowledged in abstracts/publications for your technical contribution. Know when a project needs **IRB (Institutional Review Board)** approval and de-identify data (strip student names) before it leaves the lab.

### [Clinical Application]
Rigorous simulation research leads to improved clinical protocols and better patient outcomes. Contributing to evidence-based practice (not just data collection) is how a lab moves from "we've always done it this way" to protocols proven to improve learning and, ultimately, patient care.

### [Key Terminology]
*   **Software Logs:** Digital records of every action taken in the simulator software.
*   **Standardization:** Ensuring every research participant experiences the exact same technical environment.
*   **Evidence-Based Practice:** Using the best available published evidence, rather than habit or tradition, to guide decisions about how a simulation program operates.
*   **IRB (Institutional Review Board):** The committee that reviews and approves research involving human subjects, including most simulation studies collecting learner data.

### [Exam Scenario]
**Q:** You are assisting with a research study on nurse response times. During one session, the Wi-Fi drops and the manikin lags. What is the impact?
**A:** The data for that session is compromised due to a lack of 'Technical Consistency' and may need to be excluded from the study.

**Q:** A newly published study finds that debriefing with video review improves knowledge retention more than verbal-only debriefing. How can the operations specialist contribute to evidence-based practice at their institution?
**A:** Share the findings with the education team, offer to help pilot video-assisted debriefing (e.g., configure playback stations), and help collect local outcome data to confirm the change actually improves results before it becomes standard practice.
