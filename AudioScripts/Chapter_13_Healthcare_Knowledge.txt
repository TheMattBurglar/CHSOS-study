Chapter 13: Clinical Knowledge for the Non-Clinician

You don't need a medical degree to be a tech specialist, but you do need to speak the language. This chapter is a "crash course" in anatomy and physiology.

You'll learn the ABC's of Patient Assessment: Airway, Breathing, and Circulation. When a manikin "crashes," these are the first things a team will check. You need to know how to simulate a "blocked airway" or "absent breath sounds" on your manikin. 

You also need to be familiar with real medical equipment. You'll be handling Defibrillators, Ventilators, and IV Pumps. Safety is paramount here—you are often using real medical devices on fake patients. You must ensure that "live" electricity from a defibrillator is handled safely to protect both the students and your expensive manikin. 

Finally, the chapter covers Moulage—the art of using makeup and prosthetics to create realistic wounds, bruises, or even smells. This adds that final layer of "sensory fidelity" that makes a trauma scenario truly immersive.
