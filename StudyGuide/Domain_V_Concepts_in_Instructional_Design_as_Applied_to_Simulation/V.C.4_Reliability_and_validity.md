# V.C.4: Reliability and validity

## Domain: Concepts in Instructional Design as Applied to Simulation (15%)

### [Status]: 🟢 Studied

### [Technician Perspective]
**Reliability and Validity:** Reliability is consistency (Does the sim run the same way every time?). Validity is accuracy (Does the sim measure what it's supposed to?). Specialists ensure reliability through standard operating procedures (SOPs) and technical maintenance.

### [Clinical Application]
If a simulation lacks validity (e.g., a 'healthy' manikin has a pulse of 40), the learner's clinical judgment is misled.

### [Key Terminology]
*   **Reliability:** The degree to which an assessment or measurement tool produces stable, consistent results across repeated trials, including **test-retest reliability** (same result on repeated runs) and **inter-rater reliability** (agreement between different raters scoring the same performance).
*   **Validity:** The extent to which a tool actually measures the clinical construct it claims to measure — i.e., whether the reading or score is accurate, not just repeatable.
*   **Reliable ≠ Valid:** A tool can be perfectly reliable (consistent) without being valid (accurate) — e.g., a scale that is always off by 5 lbs is reliable but not valid. A tool cannot be valid without first being reliable.

### [Exam Scenario]
**[Analysis Level] Q:** Center A's manikin BP sensor is miscalibrated so every reading comes back exactly 20 mmHg above the true value, every single time the scenario is run. Center B's BP sensor gives a different, unpredictable reading each time the identical scenario is re-run under identical conditions. Which center has a reliability problem, and which has a validity problem?
**A:** Center B has the **reliability** problem — its readings are not consistent across repeated trials. Center A has the **validity** problem — its readings are perfectly consistent (reliable) but systematically inaccurate, so they don't measure the true value they claim to. This is why calibration errors are usually a validity issue (a stable, wrong offset), while erratic or unpredictable sensor behavior is a reliability issue.
