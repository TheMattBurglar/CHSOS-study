# III.C: Equipment training facilitation

## Domain: Healthcare Simulation Practices, Principles, and Procedures (25%)

### [Status]: 🟢 Studied

### [Technician Perspective]
**Equipment Training Facilitation:** The SimOps specialist is the primary trainer for faculty on technical use, covering both hardware (simulators, task trainers, A/V) and software/**computers** (manikin control software like LLEAP, Muse, or SimPad; debrief playback software; the LMS used to schedule and record completion). Use standardized checklists to assess faculty competency in operating A/V and simulators. Training should include how to 'reset' a room and basic troubleshooting for both the physical manikin and the control-room computer running its scenario software.

### [Clinical Application]
Faculty confidence with technology reduces 'Psychological Noise' and allows them to focus on the learners' clinical performance.

### [Key Terminology]
*   **Train-the-Trainer:** A model where the specialist trains key faculty members who then train their peers.
*   **Competency Checklist:** A tool to ensure uniform technical skill levels.
*   **Scenario Control Software:** The computer application (e.g., LLEAP, Muse, SimPad PLUS) used to program and run a manikin's physiologic responses; faculty training must cover this separately from hands-on manikin skills like limb changes or skin repair.
*   **Technical Orientation:** A dedicated session where faculty or students are introduced to the simulation hardware and software before running a scenario independently.

### [Exam Scenario]
**Q:** A new faculty member wants to run a complex scenario alone. What is the best approach to ensure operational success?
**A:** Provide a mandatory technical orientation and use a competency checklist to verify they can handle basic manikin and A/V operations before the session.
