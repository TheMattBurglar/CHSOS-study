# IV.K: Psychological and physical safety

## Domain: Professional Role: Behavior, Capabilities, and Leadership (15%)

### [Status]: 🟢 Studied

### [Technician Perspective]
**Psychological and Physical Safety:** Foster a 'Safe to Fail' environment where learners feel psychologically safe to make mistakes. Manage confidentiality and data security to maintain trust. Keep the environment **non-threatening**: use a neutral, calm tone over the control-room mic, avoid "gotcha" cues designed to catch learners off guard, and make clear that recordings are for learning, not punishment. Actively support **encouraging discussion**: if co-facilitating or taking notes, use open-ended prompts rather than yes/no questions, and make sure quieter learners get space to speak during debrief. Ensure the lab is physically safe, including **infection control** (standard precautions when handling moulage blood/body fluid simulants, cleaning and disinfecting manikins and reusable task trainers between groups per manufacturer guidelines, proper disposal of soiled single-use props), electrical safety, and trip hazards.

### [Clinical Application]
Psychological safety is the foundation of learning. If a student feels 'shamed' in simulation, they are less likely to learn and may develop negative associations with clinical practice. Consistent infection control practices in the lab also model real clinical standard precautions, reinforcing habits learners must carry into patient care.

### [Key Terminology]
*   **Safe Container:** The psychological space created by facilitators where learners feel safe to take risks.
*   **Psychological Fidelity:** The degree to which the learner feels the same pressure/emotions as in a real clinical event.
*   **Non-Threatening Environment:** A tone and setup (neutral cueing, no surprise "gotcha" moments, clear learning-not-punishment framing) that keeps stress focused on the clinical scenario, not on fear of the observers or equipment.
*   **Infection Control:** Standard precautions applied to simulation equipment and props (cleaning/disinfecting manikins and task trainers between users, proper handling and disposal of moulage/body-fluid simulants) to prevent cross-contamination between learner groups.

### [Exam Scenario]
**Q:** A student is crying after a particularly intense simulation. What is the operational and facilitator priority?
**A:** Provide immediate psychological support, ensure the debriefing focuses on learning rather than blame, and verify that the 'Fiction Contract' was not breached.

**Q:** Between back-to-back sessions, the same manikin was used for a moulage-heavy hemorrhage scenario with simulated blood. The next group is scheduled to start in five minutes. What must the operations specialist prioritize?
**A:** Physical safety through infection control: clean and disinfect the manikin and any reusable surfaces per manufacturer guidance and dispose of soiled single-use moulage materials before the next group begins, even if it means requesting a short delay.
