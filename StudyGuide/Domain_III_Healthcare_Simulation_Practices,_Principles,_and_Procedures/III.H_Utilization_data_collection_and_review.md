# III.H: Utilization data collection and review

## Domain: Healthcare Simulation Practices, Principles, and Procedures (25%)

### [Status]: 🟢 Studied

### [Technician Perspective]
**Utilization Data Collection & Review:** Track every booked hour against every actually-used hour for each room, manikin, and task trainer, not just headcount of sessions run. Log this in your scheduling system or a dedicated tracking sheet, then review it on a regular cadence (monthly or quarterly) with stakeholders like the steering committee, education director, or department chairs so purchasing, staffing, and space decisions are backed by data instead of anecdote.

### [Clinical Application]
Utilization data is what justifies (or denies) new equipment purchases, additional staff, and expanded hours, and it's also a documentation point accreditation bodies look for as evidence of good stewardship of program resources.

### [Key Terminology]
*   **Utilization Rate:** The percentage of available room/equipment time that was actually booked and used, often tracked to identify both underused assets and dangerously over-booked ones.
*   **Waitlist Data:** Records of requests turned away or delayed due to lack of capacity, a strong data point for justifying expansion.
*   **Steering Committee:** The group of stakeholders and institutional leaders, often including finance and risk management representatives, who use utilization data to guide budget and strategic decisions.

### [Exam Scenario]
**Q:** The steering committee is deciding whether to fund a second high-fidelity manikin. What data should the specialist present to make the strongest case?
**A:** Utilization rate for the existing manikin over the past year alongside documented waitlist or turned-away requests, showing sustained demand that the current inventory cannot meet.

### [Notes & Lab Application]
*   Log actual start/end times for sessions, not just the scheduled block, since sessions that run short or long affect real utilization.
*   Watch for a utilization rate that's too high (e.g., above 85 to 90 percent) as a sign there's no buffer left for preventive maintenance or staff development.
