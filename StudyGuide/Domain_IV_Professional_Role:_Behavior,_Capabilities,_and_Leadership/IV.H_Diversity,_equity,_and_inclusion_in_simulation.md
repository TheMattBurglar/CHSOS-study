# IV.H: Diversity, equity, and inclusion in simulation

## Domain: Professional Role: Behavior, Capabilities, and Leadership (15%)

### [Status]: 🟢 Studied

### [Technician Perspective]
**Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion (DEI):** Ensure manikins and task trainers represent diverse populations, most notably **simulator skin tone** (use silicone overlays or cosmetic techniques with a wax barrier to add darker skin tones to a lighter-default manikin, and stock a range of moulage skin-tone palettes) so learners practice recognizing clinical signs like cyanosis or jaundice across all skin tones. Address **religious considerations** proactively: coordinate modesty draping and, where feasible, examiner-gender preferences for SPs who observe religious modesty norms; be aware of scenarios involving religious refusal of treatment (e.g., a Jehovah's Witness declining a blood transfusion) and portray them accurately and respectfully rather than as a "trick" objective; avoid scheduling high-stakes sessions on major religious holidays when possible. Address **gender identity** directly: use a transgender or nonbinary SP's or learner's correct name and pronouns in briefing materials and verbally, don't assume a patient's gender identity from a manikin's factory-default anatomy, and ensure case materials can reflect transgender health scenarios (e.g., a transgender patient case) using affirming, accurate terminology. Recognize that bias in simulation design can lead to bias in real-world clinical care. Techs control the 'Physical Representation' of the patient.

### [Clinical Application]
Representation matters: Training on diverse manikin skins prepares clinicians to recognize clinical signs (like cyanosis) in patients of all skin tones. Handling religious and gender-identity considerations respectfully in simulation prepares learners to provide equitable, patient-centered care to real patients whose beliefs or identity differ from the "default" case template.

### [Key Terminology]
*   **Representation:** The degree to which simulation materials reflect the diversity of the real patient population.
*   **Implicit Bias:** Unconscious attitudes or stereotypes that affect our understanding and actions.
*   **Cultural Humility:** An ongoing, respectful posture toward patients and colleagues whose culture, religion, or identity differs from one's own, rather than assuming a single "correct" norm.
*   **Religious Considerations:** Accommodations (modesty draping, examiner preference, scheduling, accurate portrayal of faith-based treatment decisions) made so a case or lab policy does not disregard or misrepresent a participant's or simulated patient's religious beliefs.
*   **Gender Identity:** A person's internal sense of their own gender, which may differ from the sex characteristics assigned to a manikin or listed on a chart; respecting it means using correct names/pronouns and not defaulting case content to binary assumptions.

### [Exam Scenario]
**Q:** All the manikins in the lab are of the same skin tone. Why is this a clinical training issue?
**A:** It limits the learners' ability to recognize dermatological signs across different ethnicities, potentially leading to diagnostic bias in real practice.

**Q:** A case is written as a transgender male patient (he/him) presenting for a routine exam, but the SP briefing sheet and the confederate nurse's script both default to "she/her." What should the operations specialist do?
**A:** Correct the briefing materials and coach the SP/confederate to consistently use the patient's correct name and pronouns before the session runs, since inconsistent, non-affirming case materials undermine both the clinical accuracy and the inclusiveness of the scenario.

**Q:** An SP for a physical exam case has informed the lab that religious modesty requirements mean she prefers a female examiner and full draping between exam steps. How should the operations specialist and facilitator respond?
**A:** Honor the accommodation where feasible (schedule a female learner or examiner, brief the group on draping expectations) without compromising the case's core learning objectives, and confirm the plan with the SP in advance.
