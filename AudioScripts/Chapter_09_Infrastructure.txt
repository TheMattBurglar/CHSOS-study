Chapter 9: Designing the Perfect Lab

Building a simulation center is about more than just putting a bed in a room. It's about "Environmental Fidelity"—making the space feel real so students forget they are in a school.

The Layout: You need "Zones." A reception area for greeting, classrooms for orientation, and the simulation rooms themselves. 

Acoustics are Critical: You can't have sound leaking between rooms. If a student in Room A hears a "code blue" in Room B, it ruins the immersion. This requires special wall construction and door gaskets.

The Control Room: This is the "brain" of the center. It should have a one-way mirror looking into the simulation room, but it must be soundproof. This is where the tech specialist controls the manikin and the A/V system.

Storage: Never underestimate storage! You need space for extra manikins, beds, and mountains of supplies. A good rule of thumb is that twenty percent of your center's square footage should be dedicated to storage. 

From "Ward Rooms" with multiple beds to specialized "Wet Labs" for surgical practice, every inch of a simulation center should be designed to support the mission of patient safety.
